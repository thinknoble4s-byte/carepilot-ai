Agentic workflow and architecture for patient follow-up and continuity of care. Matches prototype v7.1, locked for the Round 1 submission.

The agent runs the routine. People make the calls.

The CarePilot agent works the follow-up list continuously: reminders, booking help, report chases, refill nudges and the morning department lists. It acts only through the same logged actions a coordinator would use, within an autonomy policy set by the clinical lead. The moment a patient mentions a health matter, it stops and hands over. Coordinators handle exceptions, escalations and conversations; doctors make every clinical decision.

Agent does it, logged

  • Booking, visit and missed-item reminders
  • Refill reminders and the pharmacy list
  • First report chase; document requests
  • Morning lists to department owners
  • Logging button replies

Agent prepares, coordinator approves

  • Structuring free-text and voice replies
  • Matching incoming documents to patients
  • Pause proposals from patient replies
  • Lost-to-follow-up closure drafts

People only

  • Anything about symptoms, mood or distress
  • Verifying documents; phone calls
  • Closing a case; exceptions to the rules
  • Doctor: urgent or routine, and the instruction

How the design bar is enforced

The Health-a-thon puts clinical AI out of scope. An agent that works on its own makes this more important, not less: each exclusion needs a place in the architecture where it is structurally blocked, and an eval that proves it.

Out of scopeWhere it could creep inHow the architecture blocks it
DiagnosisStructuring a patient's reply about pain at the surgery siteThe extraction schema has no severity, cause or diagnosis fields. A validator rejects any output containing them, and a red-team eval set runs before every release.
Treatment recommendationsCoordinator copilot drafting a call briefThe copilot reads contact history and order status only. It cannot edit the plan and has no tool that writes to the patient.
Clinical decision supportDoctor's "Needs my decision" screenShows the patient's words, the translation and the source links. No suggested action; the doctor picks urgent or routine with nothing pre-selected.
Clinical risk scoringWorkspace orderingSorted by due date and rule only. Early-warning facts ("last message unanswered", "refill late") are listed as facts and never added up into a score.
Interpretation of medical dataReading an outside report; noticing a health mentionSarvam Vision extracts identifiers and metadata only. The health-matter flag decides who reads a reply, never what it means.
Autonomous clinical adviceThe agent replying to a patientThe agent never discusses health matters. On any health mention it stops, sends a fixed acknowledgement and hands over. All outbound text comes from templates the clinical lead approved.

In scope and visible: leverages existing AI (Sarvam, LangGraph); works with existing workflows (Excel register, uploaded appointment list, WhatsApp, printed department lists); assistive, because the agent works only within a clinician-set policy and every clinical step has a human gate; measurable on two KPIs within 60 to 90 days.

Live and shadow modes above are the prototype's demo settings. In the pilot, every autonomous task starts in shadow for weeks 1 to 4: the agent proposes, a coordinator approves with one tap, and each approval or rejection counts toward that task's acceptance rate. A task goes live only at 95%+ acceptance without edits, and can be moved back to shadow at any time.

Stop-and-hand-over

The moment a patient or caregiver mentions a symptom, feeling or worry, the agent stops, sends a fixed acknowledgement, and hands the conversation to the coordinator. It never discusses health matters, and it sends nothing more to that patient until a person closes the handover.

How the agent knows to stop

  1. Button taps are already structured, so the agent acts on them. A "Booked" tap is recorded even when the same reply carries a health mention.
  2. The agent never acts on the content of free text or voice. Sarvam-30B sets one flag, health matter or not, tuned to over-flag; uncertain counts as yes.
  3. Flagged replies become a handover at the top of Needs you. Unflagged ones still become approve-first proposals a coordinator reads.
  4. So a missed flag changes which queue a person sees the reply in, not whether a person sees it.

What the coordinator sees

The Workspace opens with today at a glance: actions the agent took since yesterday evening, patients fully handled by the agent, approvals waiting, and patients who need you now. Below that, one-tap agent proposals, the Needs you list, and Handled by the agent. A patient moves back to Needs you automatically if a reply does not arrive in time or a health matter comes up.

Other limits on the agent

It can be switched off for any patient from their patient view. It stops chasing at G4 and hands the patient to the named caller. Its messages are signed as the clinic's automated assistant, and automated messaging is covered in the DPDP consent. It skips paused, closed and handed-over patients.

The agent, its AI, and the services it acts through

One named agent, four AI capabilities it calls, and eight services it acts through. Anything that must behave the same way every time for a medico-legal audit is a service, not a model; the agent chooses when to use it, within policy.

Gap rules, escalation and handoff deadlines

After the ladder

Patient not reached by phone: coordinator calls the caregiver within 3 working days. When the lost-to-follow-up definition is met (60+ days since the missed item and at least four documented attempts), the agent drafts the closure. The coordinator closes the case with a reason, the clinical lead reviews it, and only then does the KPI denominator change.

Handoff deadlines

Concern decision: coordinator to doctor, 1 working day, then the doctor's cover. Instruction in the record: 1 working day after the decision, then a reminder. Urgent call: same day, then the coordinator's cover. Booking from an instruction: department owner, 2 working days, then their cover. Patient notified: automatic, same day.

Framework decisions

The design bar names LangChain, OpenClaw and Hermes Agents as examples. Each is judged on one question: can every action the agent takes be reproduced and explained to a hospital's medico-legal team?

LangGraph (LangChain)

Adopt

Runs the CarePilot agent. Explicit graphs are auditable, interrupt() maps onto approve-first and handover, and the checkpointer lets a proposal or concern wait days without losing state.

Sarvam

Adopt

The agent's Indian-language senses: speech, documents, translation. Has a self-hosted path for pilot data. Also the Health-a-thon technology partner.

OpenClaw

Borrow the pattern

Its gateway idea is right: channels separated from the agent, and the agent limited to an allow-list of tools. CarePilot's autonomy policy is that allow-list. Keep OpenClaw itself off the patient path: patient messaging must run on the WhatsApp Business Platform, and shell and browser tools are the wrong blast radius near health data.

Hermes Agent

Keep out of runtime

An agent that writes its own skills conflicts with an autonomy policy the clinical lead signs off. Fine as the developer's assistant during the build sprint, on synthetic data.

Supporting tools: Langfuse for traces and evals (self-hosted), APScheduler for the 07:00 gap check and 07:30 agent run, pytest suites for every gap rule and autonomy entry. No message bus in the MVP; a Postgres outbox is enough at one-clinic scale.

Patient-reported concern

Handover›Sent to doctor›Urgent / routine›Instruction›New order card›Closed

Outside document

Received›Unverified›VerifiedorRejected

Nothing is verified without an attached file and two matching identifiers. Never auto-promoted to the in-house trust tier.

Pause suspends rules and the agent. "Agent off" keeps the rules running but routes all contact to the coordinator. Closing always needs a person and a reason.

Trust, consent and governance

The senior oncologist's review made provenance a day-one safety requirement. An autonomous agent adds a second requirement: everything it does must be as reviewable as a person's work.

The agent is a named actor

The audit trail has four actor types: a named person, the system ("CarePilot gap check"), the CarePilot agent, and AI suggestions. Agent entries record the task, its mode, and for approve-first work, who approved. AI entries record model and prompt version.

Same actions as a coordinator

The agent has no private tools. It sends through the same template library, writes through the same order-card actions, and appears in the same audit trail. Anything a coordinator can review, they can review for the agent.

Provenance on every flag

Every status the doctor sees opens its source in one click: the report, the appointment list row, the WhatsApp message and audio. A timeline item with no originating order is shown as a gap, never assumed.

Four trust tiers

In-house feed, verified external, unverified external, patient-reported. When sources disagree, for example an outside prescription with medicines not on the hospital list, both are shown under "Sources disagree" and flagged to the doctor. Nothing is resolved automatically.

Consent and transparency

A DPDP consent record with a reference number and explicit scope (outside-record retrieval, external-care reconciliation, WhatsApp contact with patient and caregiver, automated messaging) gates every outbound action. Agent messages are signed as the clinic's automated assistant, and HELP always reaches a person. ABDM consent artefacts follow in phase 2.

WhatsApp is a channel, not a record

Every inbound message, file and call outcome is copied into the patient's record with its audit entry. Staff never work from personal phones or personal WhatsApp.

Change control

Plan templates, rule thresholds, message wording and the autonomy policy are versioned and signed off. Models and prompts ship only after passing the eval suite, including zero scope violations on the red-team set.

Access and data minimisation

Coordinator and doctor see their patients; the chief sees aggregates only. Names, phone numbers and IDs are replaced with tokens before any chat-model call. Audio and documents stay in the India-region object store.

What gets measured in 60 to 90 days

Two defensible KPIs with a baseline measured inside the clinic before go-live, as the reviewer asked. Only the aggregate leaves the clinic. Agent metrics sit underneath as operating evidence, not headline claims.

Recall completion

Share of follow-up items completed within ±14 days of due. Baseline from the clinic's own register (prototype uses a synthetic 64%, Feb to Jul 2026).

Time to first human contact

Median working days from a G4 escalation to the first call. Shows whether the coordinator time the agent frees up reaches the patients who need it.
